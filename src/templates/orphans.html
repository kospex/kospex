Orphaned Repositories

A repository is orphaned when no one who authored its code is still here: none of its authors has committed in the last year.

Authors are everyone who authored a commit to the repository, matched by author email. Merge commits and bots are not counted as authoring. A Last sync in red is more than {{ stale_days }} days ago, so that repository needs a sync before its status can be trusted.

Status
  • Orphaned - no author is still here
  • Knowledge - at least one author is still here
  • No authors - no person has authored a commit (bots may have)
Authors
- everyone (by author email) who has authored a commit to this repository, all time. Merge commits and bots are not counted.
Present
- how many of those authors committed in the last 365 days: still here. Each counts once, whether they wrote half the code or one line. A year is what research that tested the threshold chose for "has left" (Avelino et al. 2019, Calefato et al. 2022): 90 days wrongly marks many people who pause and come back as gone.
% Here
- Present as a percentage of Authors; blank when there are no authors.
Active
- how many of those authors committed in the last 90 days, so their knowledge is fresh. Present but not Active is someone still around who may need time to get back into the code.
Last commit
- the repository's latest commit, so a repository nobody has touched for years is visible.
Bots active
- bots that committed to this repository in the last 90 days. Orphaned with bots active means dependencies are still being updated with no one left who worked on it.
Last sync
- days since kospex last fetched or synced this repository. In red, it is more than {{ stale_days }} days ago: commits since then are missing, so its authors, and anyone who is only active there, may look less active than they are. It needs a sync: {{ sync_command }}
